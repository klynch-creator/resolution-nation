# Attorney Outreach — Ed-Tech / FERPA / COPPA / NY Ed Law 2-d

**Goal:** retain counsel that can (1) review the legal drafts in `legal/`, (2) red-line the SDPC NDPA template for our use, (3) advise on NYC DOE vendor submission, and (4) be on-call for the first 2–3 pilot DPAs.

**Realistic budget for Year 1:** $5–15k total legal spend, per the GTM doc. Optimize for a small-firm/solo specialist or a midsize boutique associate, not BigLaw partner-hours.

## Shortlist

Ranked by fit for a solo founder with a constrained budget. Reach out to 3–4 in parallel; whoever responds fastest with the right scoping mindset wins.

NYC-based media/tech/privacy boutique with a dedicated [Data Strategy, Privacy & Security practice](https://fkks.com/practice-areas/privacy-data-security) that explicitly covers children and minors (COPPA, Age-Appropriate Design Codes). Boutique = more flexible than BigLaw on scope and fees.

- **Why them:** real COPPA bench, NY-based, used to working with media/tech startups (their bread and butter is creator/entertainment companies). They know how to write a privacy policy that satisfies the FTC.
- **Contact path:** general inquiry through the firm site, or reach out to a privacy associate directly:
  - [Maria Nava-Gutiérrez](https://fkks.com/attorneys/maria-nava-gutierrez) — associate, Data Strategy, Privacy & Security
- **What to ask:** scoping a fixed-fee package (privacy policy + ToS + DPA template review) or a capped retainer.

### 2. Octillo Law — *Strong NY-state fit*

Buffalo-headquartered boutique focused on data security, privacy, and incident response. They've published [specifically on the FERPA/COPPA landscape for EdTech](https://octillolaw.com/insights/understanding-the-landscape-of-education-law-edtech-ferpa-coppa-and-other-considerations-2/).

- **Why them:** NY firm (admitted in-state, knows NYSED and NYC DOE), real ed-tech privacy expertise, smaller than the global firms = more accessible. Active in incident response which is valuable if you ever have a breach.
- **Contact:** Buffalo office — Liberty Building, 420 Main St., Suite 1110, Buffalo, NY 14202. General contact via [their site](https://octillolaw.com/contact/). For specifically ed-tech work, ask to be routed to the privacy team that wrote the article above.

### 3. Orrick — Emily S. Tabatabai — *Highest expertise, BigLaw rates*

Per her [bio](https://www.orrick.com/en/People/4/7/D/Emily-S-Tabatabai), Emily advises dozens of EdTech providers on FERPA, COPPA, and state student data privacy laws including SOPIPA, NY Ed Law 2-d, SOPPA, etc. She's also published on ed-tech privacy in industry forums.

### 4. F3 Law (Fagen Friedman & Fulfrost) — *The folks who built the NDPA*

California-based education law firm that [architected the SDPC National Data Privacy Agreement](https://www.f3law.com/services/education-technology/) — the very template we're recommending you use for every school contract. Largest ed-tech department of any education-focused firm in the U.S.

### 5. Andrew S. Bosin LLC — *Solo SaaS specialist, NJ/NY*

[Andrew](https://www.njbusiness-attorney.com/top-nyc-startup-lawyers-and-attorneys/) is a solo practitioner specializing in SaaS founders. He is not an ed-tech specialist, but he is SaaS-fluent and accessible to solo founders at flat-fee rates.

- **Strategy:** retain for the generic work; budget a separate specialist retainer for ed-tech specifics.

## What to ask in every scoping call

1. Have you done a NYC DOE vendor compliance submission? (If no, this is a flag — it's specific.)
2. Have you red-lined the SDPC NDPA + NY Exhibit E for a vendor before?
3. Do you offer fixed-fee packages, capped retainers, or only hourly?
4. Can you support a COPPA Safe Harbor submission to iKeepSafe?
5. Conflict check — do you represent any of: Lexia, IXL, Reading A-Z, Seesaw, MagicSchool, ClassDojo, Epic, Nearpod, Amplify? (Direct competitors)
6. What is your turnaround for initial document red-lines? (Anything > 3 weeks is a problem given pilot timeline)
7. Do you carry malpractice insurance? (Standard yes — confirm.)
